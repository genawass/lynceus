- (decision) Source P1: Preserve the goal of complete trustworthy annotations, but never promise universal ground truth; source pixels, shared model blind spots, and policy ambiguity bound confidence.
- (decision) Source P2: Use a fixed annotation policy with whole entities by default, part relationships internally, separate stuff and groups, and visible rather than amodal box extent.
- (decision) Source P3: Combine class-independent proposals, semantic inventory, concept search, exhaustive overlapping multi-scale spatial passes, and conservative candidate union before verification.
- (decision) Source P4: Preserve instance/part/group alternatives; refine masks for tight boxes; name each object at its most specific supported general class; retain unknown objects.
- (decision) Source P5: Separate existence, count, boundary, class, granularity, and omission uncertainty; blind rediscovery, residual inspection, and exemplar search address omissions.

- (decision) Source P7: Export accepted and uncertain objects, unresolved regions, search coverage, policy, model provenance, reproducible artifacts, and distinct downstream use profiles.

- (assumption) A1: The first delivery is a local Python CLI/library for still images; batch processing repeats the single-image contract. No web service or interactive annotation UI is required.

- (assumption) A7: Quality thresholds and sample sizes in evaluation.md are proposed delivery targets, not user-approved or achieved results; qualification is domain-specific and may fail.

- (decision) D23: The ontology `useful` flag is constrained by reference granularity. A class may be marked useful only if it excludes at least one other class the reference distinguishes in the declared domain; a parent covering every reference class is a tautology there, whatever its name. Useful-label coverage is always reported split into a specific and a generic share, and G4 gains a specific-label floor of 0.50 inside its 0.80 bound, so a permitted parent applied to everything cannot satisfy the coverage gate. Live case: `vehicle` in a road-user domain.
- (decision) D24: Add gate G9 granularity conformance, lower bound 0.90 on a granularity-annotated reference, measuring part/whole, group, and scene-layer agreement. CAP-2 previously had no empirical verification path and rested on synthetic fixtures the spec itself says cannot qualify anything. Where the reference does not annotate granularity, G9 is inconclusive for lack of evidence and the unmeasured policy rows are named.
- (decision) D25: The D7 panel sizes are coverage floors, not statistical sizes. WP-03 delivers a power calculation deriving required image-cluster counts per gate and stratum from the threshold, the Bonferroni-corrected alpha and the intra-image correlation, run before the locked split is opened. A gate whose required panel exceeds the collected one is declared structurally inconclusive in advance rather than reported as a bound that could never have cleared. K counts every gated metric, claimed stratum and per-domain repetition, and enters the power calculation as an input. G1 at 0.99 and G3 at 0.85 on 30 clusters are the two gates most likely to fail this check at the declared floors.

- (decision) D31: Miss attribution is a prerequisite for adding a discovery route, not an optional report. Every unmatched eligible reference is assigned to the mechanism that lost it -- below threshold, merged away, merged with a neighbour, localized below the match criterion, or never proposed at any score in any view -- and only the last bucket justifies another route. Misses are fully determined by the reference, so this diagnostic is valid on a finite-category panel where precision is not. Proposal caps are recorded per route; a route decoding a fixed query count can lose objects to cap saturation, a route emitting one prediction per patch token above a threshold cannot, and the shipped OWLv2 adapter is the second kind.
- (decision) D32: Routes, not weights. A second prompted text-conditioned detector adds no new route and buys less recall than its benchmark position suggests, while costing precision immediately because every added box enters the retained set and only some enter the matched set. Grounding DINO variants are therefore deferred: MM-Grounding-DINO is the strongest open-weights candidate (Apache-2.0, LVIS val 31.9 zero-shot) but is absent from the pinned transformers 4.49.0, so adopting it is a dependency bump that invalidates the bundle manifest and every cache key and must be staged as its own change. API-only variants (Grounding DINO 1.5/1.6, DINO-X) are excluded by the offline constraint. If miss attribution shows a real unproposed remainder, a class-independent route under D22 is preferred over a second prompted one.
- (decision) D33: Add retained-set pruning as an assessor input, with TTN (Voxel51) as the first candidate. Pruner selectivity is measurable on a finite-category panel because removals among matched boxes are exactly measurable while removals among unmatched boxes are of unknown value, and a random pruner removes both at the same rate; any gap is signal. Recorded risks before measurement: TTN scores with CLIP ViT-L-14, which shares ancestry with the OWLv2 image tower and must be disclosed under D13; its repository states no licence and distributes weights without hashes, which blocks bundling under WP-02/WP-14 though not measurement; and its classification-trained patch embeddings are expected to degrade on the small objects that dominate this panel, so selectivity is reported by size bin.
- (event) Miss attribution measured on the held-out panel (seed 1, strict ontology), reproducing the recorded result exactly at 2808 retained boxes, 646 matched, recall 0.901, precision 0.230. Of 71 misses: 55 localized below the match criterion, 9 below threshold, 4 merged with a neighbour, 1 merged away, and 2 never proposed at or above a 0.005 probe floor. All 71 fall in the two smallest size bins. The detector's own score sweeps to selectivity 0.509 as a pruner, removing 70% of unmatched boxes for 19% of matched.
- (decision) D34: The recall gap is not a detector-capacity gap, measured rather than argued. Two of 717 eligible references were never proposed, so a second detection route could return at most 0.3 points of recall against doubled inference cost and immediate precision loss. Grounding DINO variants and further proposal routes stay deferred under D31/D32 until a panel shows a materially larger unproposed remainder. Boundary refinement carries 55 of the 71 misses and becomes the highest-value recall work in WP-07.
- (decision) D35: Boundary headroom is measured before any reconciliation or refinement rule is written. A miss whose cluster already holds an observation clearing the criterion is a representative-selection failure; a miss where no observation in any view clears it needs geometry no view produced and no selection rule can return it. Writing a refinement rule before that split is a guess about its own ceiling, and the measured failure of score-weighted fusion is what that guess costs.
- (decision) D36: Selectivity 0.509 from the detector's own score is the standing bar for any external pruner, reported by size bin. A pruner that separates but does not beat that curve adds a dependency, a licence question and a shared-ancestry disclosure for nothing.
- (decision) D37: Boundary refinement by reconciliation is closed as a path, measured rather than assumed. Headroom says 39 of 71 misses already hold a box clearing IoU 0.50, but of the 18 cases where a merged member cleared it and the representative did not, no available signal separates the better box: higher score 0 cases, more magnified view 1, untruncated over truncated 0, nothing at all 17. The clearing box scores lower every time. A selection rule reaching that ceiling would prefer lower-scoring boxes under conditions only the reference reveals, which is target-label fitting. WP-07's recall value stands but requires boundaries refined against pixels, so it waits on a segmentation route rather than on a smarter merge.
- (decision) D38: TTN is rejected as a retained-set pruner for this domain, measured against the D36 bar. Best selectivity 0.119 for the classification-trained variant and 0.178 for the authors' detection-tuned one, against 0.513 for the detector's own score on the identical 2616 scoreable boxes; at equal retention the detector score keeps 412 matched boxes to TTN's 223. It loses in every size bin. The size curve confirms the D33 prediction -- 0.077 below 16 pixels rising to 0.519 above 96 -- so the mechanism is understood, but it does not rescue the result. This is a transfer failure on sub-32-pixel aerial objects, not a refutation of the paper, and it is descriptive under the out-of-domain tier. The pruner interface is retained so the next candidate costs a run rather than a rewrite.
- (decision) D39: TTN is an in-context comparator, not a free-standing scorer: it consumes ten ground-truth reference patch embeddings per class alongside the candidate. Any such component therefore needs a per-domain reference bank of human labels, which is permitted under A3 only from a development or calibration split with leakage controls. The controls are not optional here: 24 VisDrone sequences appear in both train and val, one of them in the held-out panel, so an uncontrolled reference bank leaks into the evaluation. Recorded because it changes what such a component is -- an external calibration artifact bound into the qualification identity, not a domain-agnostic filter.
- (decision) D40: The prompt vocabulary of a concept-promptable model is frozen configuration, not a free choice at call time. Ontology IDs are schema identifiers and must not be used as prompts directly, which would make the ontology's spelling a model hyperparameter and is the prompt anchoring the naming rules already forbid. The phrasing used to query each class is recorded in that class's `synonyms`, bound into the manifest and the qualification identity, and selected only on a development or calibration split, because choosing it is dataset-level prompt optimization that the constraints forbid on target data. A vocabulary tuned on the evaluation panel invalidates that panel.
- (event) SAM 3 staging is blocked: `facebook/sam3` is gated `manual` on Hugging Face and returns 401 without an approved access request, and no token is configured on this host. The pinned `transformers` 4.49.0 also carries no SAM 2 or SAM 3 support, so adopting either is a dependency bump of the kind D32 requires to be staged as its own change. SAM 1 is ungated and supported by the pinned stack, so boundary refinement was probed with it first to decide whether those costs are worth paying.
- (decision) D41: Boundary refinement against pixels is retained as the WP-07 path, measured on the held-out panel. SAM 3 box-prompted refinement inside source-resolution tiles trades five matches at IoU 0.50 for twenty-seven at 0.75 and sixteen at 0.85, moving mean IoU of matches from 0.7715 to 0.7839 and the G5 ratio from 0.331 to 0.359. That trade favours the gates scored at tight thresholds -- G1 and G4 at 0.75, G5 at 0.85 over 0.50 -- at a small cost to G2 at 0.50. SAM 1 does not qualify: its refinement is symmetric noise, 50.4% improving against 49.6% worsening with a median IoU change of +0.0004, which loses 26 references to recover 8. The difference is directional signal, not model size.
- (decision) D42: Refinement is applied unconditionally or not at all; gating on the model's own mask quality was measured and does not work. Raising the threshold removes refinements rather than selecting good ones -- one box changes at 0.90 and none at 0.95 -- so losses and gains fall together and no threshold is net positive at IoU 0.50. A self-reported quality score is not evidence about whether a refinement helps, which is the same lesson as D37's separability check in a different guise.
- (decision) D43: Refinement runs in overlapping source-resolution tiles, one model pass per tile serving every box inside it. A whole-frame pass delivers a twelve-pixel object at about six after the processor's resize, and a per-box crop pays a full encoder pass to upscale a small window; measured, the whole-frame variant is worse than the tiled one for both models. DART's backbone sharing addresses the multi-class detection loop and not this path, and being training-free it changes cost rather than results, so it belongs at deployment and cannot decide adoption.
- (decision) D44: The refinement result replicates and is adopted as the WP-07 direction. On the development panel, unchanged parameters, refinement adds 41 matches at IoU 0.75 and 34 at 0.85 against 827 references, beside 27 and 16 against 717 on the held-out panel; the G5 ratio rises on both, 0.314 to 0.361 and 0.331 to 0.359. The small loss at IoU 0.50 on the held-out panel does not replicate -- the development panel gains six there -- so it is panel noise and the tight-threshold gain is the effect. Both panels are out-of-domain and descriptive, so this establishes direction and no gate outcome. It is the first candidate in this sequence to return more than it costs, and the first to do so twice.
- (decision) D45: Boundary refinement enters the pipeline as a typed refiner adapter behind a flag, off by default and unqualified. A refined box replaces a proposed one only as a claim about the same instance; refinement may not move a box onto a different object. The proposed geometry always stays in the candidate store with the refiner's identity and its self-reported mask quality, which remains evidence and never becomes a probability field. Where refiner and proposer disagree about extent beyond a declared tolerance, the proposed box is retained as an alternative and the boundary dimension is unresolved: two models agreeing where an object is and disagreeing how far it extends is boundary uncertainty, not a silent overwrite. Loading the refiner is deferred to the point of use so a run without the flag neither imports nor requires it, keeping the pinned stack and its bundle manifest valid.
- (decision) D46: The bundle format names its adapter and is checked against that adapter's layout. Preflight hardcoded OWLv2 and its file names, so the format could not express a second model; it now carries a per-adapter required-asset set, and a bundle cannot pass by shipping the other adapter's files. The refiner is loaded only when a refiner bundle is named, so a run without refinement neither imports nor requires the newer dependency.
- (event) Measured cost of the transformers bump that SAM 3 requires: on one panel image with an identical normalized-pixel hash and every other input unchanged, 4.49.0 retains 394 objects and 5.17.0 retains 382, with zero boxes identical between them. Integrating refinement into one process therefore requires either two dependency sets and two processes, or adopting 5.17.0 and re-measuring the baseline, the ablations and the gain on it. - (decision) D47: SAM 3's licence is Meta's SAM License dated 19 November 2025, not Apache-2.0; SAM 1 and SAM 2 were Apache-2.0 and SAM 3 is not. - (event) Re-measurement on transformers 5.17.0 complete, discharging the obligation D48 accepted. - (decision) D49: A small exhaustively annotated panel is specified as the way past the finite-category ceiling, which blocks precision, calibration and G9 together and which no amount of further finite-category data relieves. Exhaustive means annotated to the declared policy's standard rather than to a category list, with the ontology's excluded_by_convention entries the only permitted absences. Five obligations make it verifiable: annotate blind before predictions are loaded, sweep overlapping review tiles and record every visit so completeness is a property of the procedure rather than of confidence, record ambiguity instead of resolving it by fiat and keep those instances in recall but out of precision denominators, tag granularity so G9 is scoreable at all, and carry leakage identity so the panel can be checked against development or calibration data already used including an in-context reference bank. Twenty to thirty images suffices for what it unblocks; it is not a qualification panel and a gate claim still needs the panel the power calculation sizes.
- (decision) D50: Reference annotation gets a local review page, generated per image and opened over file:// beside a copy of that image, with no server, no network and no external dependency, so the panel is built under the same offline conditions the annotator requires. It is organised around the tile sweep rather than the image, zooming each planned tile to fill the viewport and recording which were reviewed, because completeness is a property of the procedure and ingestion refuses a panel whose sweep record is incomplete. It collects exactly the fields the protocol requires and shows no prediction and no run directory, which the tests assert rather than assume. This page is distinct from report.html, which explains a finished run and is forbidden from requesting annotations; this one exists outside the annotator's inference path. - (decision) D51: Prediction-assisted verification is added as a distinct artifact beside the blind exhaustive panel, because annotating a dense panel from raw pixels is expensive enough that it may not happen and an uncompleted protocol measures nothing. It adjudicates every retained box, so precision and class correctness become measurements rather than the lower bound a finite-category reference allows -- which is the question that was actually blocked. It cannot establish recall: every reference in it is by construction something the system proposed, so recall against it is trivially near one, and an object the system missed is as invisible as before. It degrades geometry where a box is accepted unchanged, since that box is the model's, so accepted and adjusted geometry are recorded separately. Added objects are kept and counted but do not make the panel exhaustive, because attention anchored by existing boxes is not a sweep. Such a panel declares anchoring prediction_assisted with exhaustive false and recall_interpretable false, and the evaluator reports its recall and coverage as anchored rather than as measurements so a later reader cannot mistake which question it answered.
- (decision) D52: Merging reconciles observations whose labels differ but share a useful common ancestor, emitting that ancestor and keeping both specific readings as alternatives; this becomes the default. The defect it fixes was misattributed: an under-merge of 619 cross-class pairs on the held-out panel was treated as geometry when it is taxonomy, because naming runs per observation and two individually confident views never fall back to the class they jointly support. Measured on both panels, it removes about two thirds of those pairs and about twelve per cent of all boxes, raising precision from 0.2335 to 0.2658 and from 0.2391 to 0.2643, at a cost of eleven matches at IoU 0.50 on each panel: when two differently labelled boxes merge the higher-scoring one survives and sometimes the discarded one was better localized, which D37 already established no available signal can prevent. Adopted because a duplicate is a false positive by construction under one-to-one matching, the relative precision gain exceeds the relative recall loss, and the generic `vehicle` share barely moves, so the gain is not bought with unspecific labels. `unknown_object` is excluded from the rule.
- (decision) D53: Precision is recovered by auditing a sample of unmatched boxes rather than by annotating a panel. A matched box is a true positive and needs no judgement, so the ambiguity a finite-category reference creates is confined to unmatched boxes, and the estimated proportion of those that are real converts the precision bound into an interval, the uncorrected figure being the case where that proportion is zero. The sample is drawn under a frozen seed and stratified by object size and emitted class so a rare class or a small-object regime cannot be accidentally absent, and the draw is recorded so it can be re-judged or extended rather than silently redrawn. Each sampled box is judged for whether it is a real entity the policy includes and, only then, for its class, which yields classification accuracy on the one population the reference cannot adjudicate. Real, false and undecidable are reported separately and never collapsed, and the undecidable rate travels with the estimate, because an audit that could not resolve much of its sample has not measured precision however narrow its interval. An audit establishes nothing about recall.
- (decision) D54: Automatic verification replaces manual adjudication for resolving what retained boxes are, with its limits carried in the result rather than in a footnote. A second model yields agreement and not truth, but the reference already settles half the population, so the verifier's confirmation rate on known true positives measures its sensitivity on this data at this object scale. Measured with SAM 3 prompted on the ontology and tiled at source resolution: sensitivity 0.967 overall and above 0.94 in every size bin including below sixteen pixels, which refutes the failure mode that would otherwise invalidate the exercise. Confirmation on undecided boxes is 0.357, implying about 644 of 1743 are real and precision nearer 0.53 than the 0.266 bound. Those corrected figures assume equal sensitivity across both populations, which nothing establishes, so they are descriptive and are labelled as assumptions in the output.
- (decision) D55: Class comparison against an ontology is three-valued, not two. Exact agreement was 0.432, which alone reads as a naming failure; separating compatible-but-broader from genuine conflict gives 0.767 compatible and 0.233 conflict, because emitting `vehicle` where a verifier says `car` is conservative naming working rather than a contradiction. The 413 broader cases are the specificity cost G4's floor constrains, now enumerable. The 287 conflicts are concentrated on people -- 204 involve a person the system abstained on or called a vehicle -- which corroborates from a second direction the finding that `person` has no useful ancestor in this ontology, and points at the ontology rather than the detector.
- (decision) D56: D40 was wrong to put prompt phrasing in the ontology's `synonyms`, and is superseded. Phrasing is per-model: a CLIP-style text tower, a presence head and a phrase grounder do not respond alike, so one vocabulary is wrong for every model but the one it was tuned on. A vocabulary is therefore a separate versioned artifact per model family, declaring the ontology and version it maps from, the adapter it serves, and a phrasing per class, and it is bound into the manifest and qualification identity by id, version and hash. Keeping it out of the ontology is what stops a prompt recalibration from bumping the taxonomy's version and invalidating qualification results unrelated to taxonomy. An uncovered class falls back to the canonical name and the run records the fallback so an uncalibrated class is visible. Cross-model comparison uses each model's own vocabulary, since the question is whether models agree about the world and not whether they respond alike to one string; which vocabulary each side used travels with the comparison. Fitting remains development or calibration split only, as D40 required.
- (event) Reviewing the ontology found the rule D40 states already violated by shipped code, and independently duplicated. The OWLv2 adapter builds its prompts as the ontology's class names verbatim, and the SAM 3 verifier does the same in a separately written line, so the two would drift apart under any calibration and the cross-model verification already reported compared two uncalibrated vocabularies. The `synonyms` field is populated on none of the twelve classes in either ontology and is read by nothing. DART has no implementation, so it would inherit whichever prompt path exists when it is built.
- (decision) D57: A prompt vocabulary is searched rather than written by hand, under the discipline of an experiment. Fitting happens on development data only and the fitted vocabulary is frozen before a single measurement on the held-out panel, because selecting phrasing against the locked panel is the dataset-level prompt optimization the constraints forbid and it invalidates the panel; the development score is not the result. Candidates are enumerated in advance and recorded, so a vocabulary that beat two alternatives is distinguishable from one that beat forty. The search is coordinate ascent over classes with the whole vocabulary re-scored at each step, because a prompted detector emits the winning class over all prompts and per-class objectives optimized independently would not compose; it finds a local optimum and claims no more. Evaluation reuses cached image features and box predictions, which do not depend on the text, so a candidate costs a text-tower pass and a matrix multiply -- a cost reduction rather than an approximation, since the scores match a full forward pass. The development-to-held-out gap is reported, because selecting among candidates on a small panel fits noise and a search that gains on one and loses on the other has found noise.
